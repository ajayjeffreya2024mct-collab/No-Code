# Experiment 8: Case Study on the Business Impact of a No-Code Application Implementation

## 1. Aim

To study and evaluate the business impact of implementing a no-code application, using measurable indicators such as time, cost, productivity, accuracy, and user satisfaction.

## 2. Objectives

- Understand how no-code adoption affects business processes.
- Define baseline and post-implementation metrics.
- Calculate cost savings, return on investment (ROI), and payback period.
- Identify benefits, risks, and lessons learned.

## 3. Requirements

- A real or hypothetical organization with a manual or spreadsheet-based process
- Spreadsheet software for calculations
- Optional: published vendor case studies (for example, from Zoho, Microsoft Power Apps, or Airtable customer stories) for reference

> **Important note on data:** The scenario and figures below are **illustrative and hypothetical**, created to demonstrate the method. For your submission, replace them with data from a real organization, an interview, or a cited public case study.

## 4. Theory

### 4.1 Why Measure Business Impact

Technology investments must justify themselves. Impact assessment compares the situation **before** and **after** implementation using agreed metrics.

### 4.2 Typical Impact Categories

| Category | Example Metrics |
|---|---|
| Time | Process cycle time, time to build, time to respond |
| Cost | Development cost, license cost, manual effort cost |
| Productivity | Tasks per employee per day, hours saved per week |
| Quality | Error rate, rework rate, data accuracy |
| Experience | User satisfaction (CSAT), adoption rate |
| Agility | Time to implement a change request |
| Governance | Audit trail availability, compliance incidents |

### 4.3 Key Formulas

```
Net benefit      = Total benefits - Total costs
ROI (%)          = (Net benefit / Total costs) x 100
Payback period   = Initial investment / Monthly net benefit
Hours saved      = (Old time per task - New time per task) x Number of tasks
Cost saved       = Hours saved x Average hourly cost
```

## 5. Case Study (Illustrative): Leave and Expense Approval at a Mid-Sized Company

### 5.1 Background

**Organization:** A hypothetical manufacturing company with 300 employees.

**Problem:** Leave requests and expense claims were submitted through email and spreadsheets, causing:

- Delayed approvals (average 4 days)
- Lost or duplicate requests
- Manual consolidation by HR and Finance each month
- No real-time visibility of balances or status

### 5.2 Solution

The company built a **no-code approval application** on a platform such as Zoho Creator, with:

- Online forms for leave and expense requests
- Multi-level approval workflow (manager, then HR/Finance)
- Automatic email notifications
- Dashboard for HR and management
- Integration with the existing spreadsheet/payroll export

Built by one HR analyst (citizen developer) with part-time IT guidance.

### 5.3 Implementation Summary

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Build time | 3 weeks (part-time) |
| Pilot | 1 department, 2 weeks |
| Rollout | Company-wide after pilot |
| Training | One-hour session plus quick guide |

### 5.4 Before and After Metrics (Hypothetical)

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average approval time | 4 days | 1 day | 75% faster |
| HR monthly consolidation effort | 40 hours | 8 hours | 32 hours saved |
| Error/duplicate rate | 6% | 1% | 5 percentage points lower |
| Requests with visible status | 0% | 100% | Full visibility |
| Time to change a form field | 2 weeks (IT ticket) | 1 hour | Much faster |
| Employee satisfaction (1 to 5) | 2.8 | 4.2 | +1.4 |

### 5.5 Cost-Benefit Analysis (Hypothetical)

**Costs (first year)**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Platform subscription | 1,200 |
| Build effort (3 weeks part-time, about 60 hours at 20/hour) | 1,200 |
| Training and support | 600 |
| **Total cost** | **3,000** |

**Benefits (first year)**

| Item | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| HR consolidation time saved | 32 hours x 12 months x 20/hour | 7,680 |
| Reduced rework from errors | Estimated | 1,500 |
| Manager and employee time saved on follow-ups | Estimated | 3,000 |
| **Total benefit** | | **12,180** |

*(Currency units are arbitrary; use your local currency.)*

**Results**

```
Net benefit    = 12,180 - 3,000 = 9,180
ROI            = (9,180 / 3,000) x 100 = 306%
Monthly net benefit (average) = 12,180 / 12 = 1,015
Payback period = 3,000 / 1,015 = about 3 months
```

### 5.6 Qualitative Impacts

- Employees gained transparency and faster responses.
- HR shifted from clerical work to analysis and employee support.
- Business teams gained confidence to request and build more tools.
- An audit trail improved compliance readiness.

### 5.7 Challenges and Risks Observed

| Challenge | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Shadow IT concerns | IT governance policy, approved platform list |
| Data privacy of employee records | Role-based access, data-sharing review |
| Dependence on one citizen developer | Documentation, second owner, backup |
| Platform subscription growth with users | Annual license review |
| Integration limits with legacy payroll | CSV/API export, phased integration |

### 5.8 Lessons Learned

1. Start with a small, high-pain process.
2. Involve end users early through pilots.
3. Establish governance and ownership from day one.
4. Track baseline metrics **before** building so impact can be proven.
5. Plan for maintenance and change requests.
